TARGET SCALE

Consider the area of each target's tight visible bounding rectangle relative to the full image area. Use the rectangle around the visible portion, without extending it to reconstruct hidden parts.

Scale is geometric. Do not increase this score for blur, noise, or unusual appearance. A narrow object may have small area even when it is long.

Level 1: A target occupies at least 10% of the image area.
Level 2: A target occupies at least 3% but less than 10% of the image area.
Level 3: A target occupies at least 1% but less than 3% of the image area.
Level 4: A target occupies at least 0.25% but less than 1% of the image area.
Level 5: A target occupies less than 0.25% of the image area.

Return the image-wide median level. Do not select the level from the largest or smallest target alone.

TARGET VISIBILITY

Consider how much of each target is hidden behind another object or cut off by the image boundary.

Judge missing physical parts, not missing detail from small size or image degradation. An ordinary viewpoint showing one side of an object is not itself occlusion.

Level 1: The target is fully visible, with no meaningful part hidden or cut off.
Level 2: A small part is hidden or cut off, while nearly all of the target remains visible.
Level 3: A substantial part is hidden or cut off, while most of the target remains visible.
Level 4: Most of the target is hidden or cut off, leaving only a few parts visible.
Level 5: Nearly the entire target is hidden or cut off, leaving only a small fragment visible.

Return the image-wide median level. In the evidence, distinguish occlusion from truncation when either is present.

INSTANCE SEPARATION

Consider whether each target can be distinguished as one separate instance from nearby targets and background structures.

Judge whether its visible boundaries can be assigned to that instance rather than confused with surrounding boundaries. Do not score how much of the object is hidden; that belongs to visibility.

A partly occluded object can still be clearly separate. A dense group can contain individually clear objects. Overlapping bounding rectangles alone do not establish poor separation.

Level 1: The target's visible boundaries are distinct from surrounding content.
Level 2: The target touches nearby content, but its visible boundaries remain clear.
Level 3: Some visible boundaries intermingle with surrounding content, making their assignment ambiguous in places.
Level 4: Many visible boundaries intermingle, leaving substantial ambiguity about the target's separate extent.
Level 5: The target merges with surrounding content so strongly that its separate extent is largely unresolved.

Return the image-wide median level. Describe the competing targets or background structures in the evidence. Do not increase the level merely because there are many objects.

IMAGE DEGRADATION

Consider imaging defects affecting target-bearing regions: blur, noise, compression artifacts, underexposure, or overexposure. Include glare or shadows when they obscure recorded target detail.

Judge the loss of detail caused by these defects. Do not count small target size, occlusion, naturally smooth surfaces, or a clean drawing's lack of photographic texture as degradation.

Degradation confined to irrelevant background must not increase the level.

Level 1: No noticeable imaging defect affects the target's visible region.
Level 2: Minor imaging defects are present, but edges and surface detail remain clear.
Level 3: Imaging defects obscure some surface detail, while most edges remain clear.
Level 4: Imaging defects obscure much surface detail and substantial portions of the visible edges.
Level 5: Imaging defects obscure almost all surface detail and most of the visible outline.

Return the image-wide median level. Name the visible defect in the evidence. Local defects affect only the targets whose regions they degrade.

APPEARANCE ATYPICALITY

Consider pose, orientation, viewpoint, foreshortening, or physical deformation relative to ordinary appearances of the category.

Describe visible departures in form without guessing how common they are in detector training data. Unexpected location alone is not atypical form. Depiction style alone does not increase this level.

Do not count small size, hidden parts, or blur again here.

Level 1: The target has an ordinary pose, orientation, and shape.
Level 2: The target has a slight variation in pose or viewpoint, with familiar proportions.
Level 3: An unusual pose or viewpoint noticeably changes the target's familiar arrangement of parts.
Level 4: Strong rotation, foreshortening, or deformation substantially changes its usual outline or proportions.
Level 5: Extreme deformation or distortion leaves very little of the category's ordinary shape or arrangement of parts.

Return the image-wide median level. Explain the visible change in form rather than stating that recognition is difficult.
